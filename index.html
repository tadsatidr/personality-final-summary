Key message: many researchers say soft skills are essential for entrepreneurs and many jobs. Both are important for career success, but soft skills are necessary for most jobs. The skill needed for a job is a combination of both.

Why employers use personality tests

  • Employees placed in roles that don't match their personality are likely to have lower engagement.
  • Most tests help predict job performance, working style and other career-relevant traits.

MBTI (personality types key)

  • E Extroverts (energized by people, quick pace, multitasking) vs I Introverts (work alone/small groups, deliberate, one task)
  • S Sensors (facts, details, past experience) vs N Intuitives (possibilities, big picture, patterns)
  • T Thinkers (logic, pros and cons, fairness) vs F Feelers (sensitive, personal values, effect on others)
  • J Judgers (organized, plans, rules) vs P Perceivers (options open, spontaneous, flexible)
  • Helps hiring but doesn't depict career performance; gauges strengths and points to improve

1.6 Answering common interview questions

1. Self-introduction template

  • Greeting + name → graduated from [university] with a degree in [major]
  • Activities → improved interpersonal and teamwork skills
  • Internship at [company] → hands-on experience in [skills]
  • What you learned → eager to learn, open to challenges, can contribute → thank you

“Tell me about yourself” (slide model)

  • Thank them for the opportunity
  • Describe yourself: confident, resilient, reliable, enjoys working with different people
  • “I am a very positive person who believes you only get out of life what you are prepared to put in.”

2. Strengths: Strength → Example → Result

  • Strength: stay calm under pressure, communicate well with customers
  • Example: internship: unhappy customer about a delay → listened, apologized sincerely, coordinated with the team
  • Result: the customer came back; the strength fits a customer-facing role
  • Short lines: punctuality · fast learner · leadership · collaborative · optimistic

3. Weakness: 3 steps

  • Choose a weakness that does not significantly affect the position
  • Explain the steps you are taking to improve it
  • Demonstrate the progress you have made
  • Ex 1: overly detail-oriented → internal deadlines and checklists
  • Ex 2: nervous presenting in English → rehearse, record voice, training → now fluent with positive feedback

The STAR method (behavioural questions)

S: Situation
set the scene: who, what, when, where→T: Task
your responsibility or goal→A: Action
exact steps you took (“I”, not “we”)→R: Results
positive outcome, numbers, what you learned
Slide example: hotel front desk (STAR)
  • S: a guest was unhappy because the room looked larger in the website photos
  • T: as receptionist, ensure satisfaction and resolve it promptly
  • A: apologized, listened, checked the reservation, offered a free upgrade and a complimentary fruit basket
  • R: the guest complimented the service in the feedback survey; the hotel's review score that month improved
Practice question list (from the last slide)
  • Tell me about yourself.
  • Why did you apply for this job?
  • Where do you see yourself in five years?
  • What motivates you to do a good job? · What makes you interested in this field?
  • What is your greatest strength? · What are your key skills?
  • Describe a weakness / an area to improve. How are you working on it?
  • How would you describe your personality?
  • How do you deal with pressure / handle stress / manage your time / respond to feedback?

Closing slide: “A job interview is not a test of your knowledge, but your ability to use it at the right time.”

2.1 What is ethics?

Two definitions on the slides

  • A branch of philosophy concerned with human conduct, especially the behaviour of individuals in society.
  • The study of what people ought to do, and why, when choices affect others.

Three parts of ethical thinking

  • Right & wrong: is the action fair and responsible?
  • People & impact: consider others, not only personal gain
  • Reasons: explain and defend the decision

Philosophical foundation: Aristotle

  • “Virtue is developed through habit and practice.”
  • Virtue: good character (honesty, courage, justice)
  • Golden Mean: a balanced, moderate action (avoid extremes)
  • Eudaimonia: human flourishing, a truly good life (long-term well-being)

Likely exam questions

Likely QDifferentiate manners and etiquette, with a table example of each.

Use the table in 5.1. Example: thanking the waiter (manners) vs bread plate on the left (etiquette). Best: manners + etiquette = confident and respectful anywhere.

Likely QDescribe a formal Western place setting.

Charger centre; forks left (salad/fish outer, dinner inner); knife (blade in) and soup spoon right; bread plate + butter knife top left; dessert spoon/fork top centre; glasses top right (water above the knife). Rules: outside in; b and d.

Likely QCompare American and Continental dining styles.

Both cut fork-left/knife-right. American: switch the fork to the right hand, tines up; rest knife across top. Continental: fork stays left, tines down; rest crossed or inverted V.

Likely QWhat is the silent service code?

Inverted V = still eating; parallel at 4 or 12 o'clock, blade in = finished.

Likely QGive dining customs of five cultures.

Pick five rows of the table in 5.4, e.g. Thailand (spoon to mouth), Japan (no upright chopsticks), Korea (wait for the eldest), India (right hand), Italy (don't cut spaghetti).

Likely QWhat should a guest and a host do at a business meal?

Guest: early 5–15 min, mid-price, no messy food, moderate drinking, thank-you within 24 h. Host: book and check dietary needs, arrive first, best seat to guest, inviter pays discreetly. Phone off the table; no food photos.

Last-minute cheat sheet

One page for the night before.

1 · Skills & 5Q

  • Hard = technical, measurable, certificates · Soft = “human skills”, personality-linked
  • Job needs a combination; soft skills needed for most jobs
  • IQ think (~20% of success) · EQ feel · MQ moral · RQ bounce back · SQ relationships
  • EQ = self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skills
  • Aptitude = talent/potential · Attitude = opinion/character

1 · Interview

  • Before: dress (slightly over), 2 resumé copies, research mission/values, prep strength = weakness length, 10–15 min early, greet all, breathe, phone off
  • During: Duchenne smile · eye contact · front half of chair · let them lead · relax · pause + examples · no fillers · don't criticize ex-boss · mirror · no fidgeting · ask Qs · thank-you ≤ 24 h
  • Strength → Example → Result · Weakness: harmless → steps → progress
  • STAR: Situation, Task, Action (“I”), Results (numbers)

2 · Ethics

  • Ethics = what we ought to do, and why · Morals = personal beliefs · Law = enforced rules
  • Aristotle: virtue · golden mean · eudaimonia
  • Hippocratic: do no harm, confidentiality, power responsibly, beneficence · ANA: autonomy, beneficence, justice, nonmaleficence
  • 6 values: honesty, integrity (words = actions), fairness (no double standards), confidentiality, respect, accountability
  • Decide: identify → options → consequences → decide → reflect
  • Challenges: conflict of interest, data privacy, misleading claims, unfair treatment · Causes: omnipotence, cultural numbness, justified neglect

3 · Entrepreneur

  • entreprendre = to undertake · vision, innovation, organization, risk · leadership > ownership
  • vs Businessman: creates market, low competition, high risk, market leader, unconventional
  • Types: small · scalable startup · large company · social
  • Components: entrepreneur · opportunity (pulled = needs / pushed = resources) · resources · organization · environment
  • Importance: jobs, innovation, society, living standard, R&D

3 · Presentation

  • Content (inform, persuade, inspire, entertain, express, instruct) → Design → Deliver
  • Top: first 7 seconds (question / stat 55-38-7 / quote) · Body: key points, rule of 3 · Bottom: end strong (longest memory)
  • 6: stance (whole body; economy gestures) · eye contact (look-lock-talk; L-R-C) · tone (head/mouth/chest) · pace (stretch/compress) · pause · non-words

4 · Business etiquette

  • Greet: stand, eyes, smile, handshake, say who + what you do, hierarchy (junior → senior), get the name right
  • Handshake: firm not crushing, dry palm, 2–3 shakes from elbow, say name · lock-grip-squeeze-shake-eye/smile-speak
  • Cards: right/both hands, facing them, read + store; never sit on, leave, refuse, or deal like a casino
  • Wai: monks/Buddha > parents/teachers/boss/elders > younger/customers
  • Stand: shoulders back, feet shoulder-width, no locked knees · Walk: head up, core, swing arms, heel-to-toe

5 · Dining

  • Manners = heart, universal · Etiquette = rules, changes by place
  • Outside in · forks L, knives/spoons R · dessert on top · bread L, drinks R · water glass above knife
  • Napkin: lap → chair (leaving) → left of plate (end)
  • American: switch fork, tines up · Continental: fork left, tines down
  • Inverted V = resting · parallel 4/12 o'clock = finished
  • Small talk: travel, food, books, hobbies; not politics, religion, money, health

5 · World & business meals

  • TH spoon to mouth · JP no upright chopsticks · CN don't flip fish · KR wait for eldest · IN/ME right hand · FR no ketchup · IT don't cut spaghetti · US tip 15–20%
  • Invitation → RSVP → dress code → thank-you ≤ 24 h
  • Guest: 5–15 min early, mid price, no messy food · Host: inviter pays, discreetly; best seat to guest
  • Phone off the table; no food photos
